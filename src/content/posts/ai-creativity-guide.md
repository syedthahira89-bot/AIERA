Have you ever stared at a blank page, a blank canvas or a silent piano and thought, *"Where do I even start?"* Every creative person knows that feeling. Here's the hopeful news: AI tools can be a wonderful partner for exactly that moment.

Not a replacement for your imagination, but a co-pilot. Someone to bounce ideas off at midnight, who never gets tired of "what if…?"

## A creative partner, not a substitute

Think of AI like a very well-read friend who's always happy to brainstorm. They can suggest a dozen directions in seconds. But they don't know your story, your taste or what makes your work feel like *you*. That part is yours.

The most rewarding way to use AI creatively is to let it widen your options, then trust your own judgement to choose, shape and finish.

## Five friendly ways to use AI in your creative work

### 1. Break the blank page

Ask for ten quick starting points: opening lines for a story, themes for a painting series, names for a café, angles for a blog post. Most won't be right, and that's the point. Seeing options you *don't* like often shows you what you *do* want.

> **Try this:** "Give me 10 unusual opening lines for a short story about a lighthouse keeper. Make each one a different mood."

### 2. Explore "what if?"

AI is great at remixing. What if your poem were written as a letter? What if your logo idea used only circles? What if your song moved from a minor key to a major one in the chorus? Quick experiments that would take hours by hand take seconds, so you can explore more freely.

### 3. Build a mood board

Image tools can sketch rough visual ideas: a colour palette for a bedroom, the feel of a book cover, the lighting for a photo shoot. Use them like a mood board, not a final piece. They're brilliant for showing a friend or client what's in your head. (Curious how they work? See [how diffusion models paint from static](/blog/diffusion-noise-to-image/).)

### 4. Get gentle feedback

Paste in a paragraph and ask: *"What's unclear here? Where does the energy drop?"* You don't have to agree with the feedback, but a fresh point of view, any time of day, can help you see your own work differently.

### 5. Handle the boring bits

Formatting, resizing images, summarising research notes, tidying a messy list of ideas. Let AI take on the repetitive chores so you have more time and energy for the parts you love.

## Keeping your own voice

The more you rely on AI's first suggestion, the more your work can start to sound like everyone else's. A few habits help keep it personal:

- **Start with your own idea**, even a rough one, before asking AI anything.
- **Edit everything.** Treat AI output as clay, not a finished sculpture.
- **Add what only you know:** your memories, your sense of humour, the details from your life.
- **Read it aloud.** If it doesn't sound like you, change it until it does.

## Creating kindly and fairly

Creativity has always been a conversation with other people's work, and AI adds some new questions. A few thoughtful habits:

- **Be open** when AI played a big part, especially in work you share or sell.
- **Respect other artists.** Avoid asking for work "in the style of" a living artist to pass it off as theirs, and never present AI-generated work as someone else's.
- **Check the rules.** Competitions, publishers and clients often have their own policies on AI-assisted work, and image rights vary by tool and country.

## Common questions

**Is AI-assisted art "real" art?**
People have asked this about photography, synthesisers and digital painting. What makes something meaningful is usually the intention, choices and care behind it, and those still come from you.

**Will using AI make me less creative?**
It depends on how you use it. Letting it think *for* you can make you rusty. Letting it help you think *more*, by generating options you then judge and shape, can make you more adventurous.

**Which tool should I start with?**
Start with a general chatbot for words and ideas. It's free to try, and the skills you learn, like asking clear questions and editing the results, carry over to every other tool.

## Try it this week

Pick one small creative project: a birthday poem, a new recipe, a weekend sketch. Use AI for just one step, maybe the brainstorm, and do the rest yourself. Notice how it felt. Did it help you start? Did it spark something new?
