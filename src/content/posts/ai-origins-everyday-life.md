Artificial intelligence can feel like it arrived overnight. In reality it is an idea more than 75 years old, and it has been quietly filtering your email, estimating your arrival time and translating menus for years. Here is where it came from, how it is making life easier for people and the planet, and how you can put it to work yourself.

## Where AI came from

![Illustrated timeline of AI history: a mathematician writing at a desk, a room-sized vintage computer, a chessboard, a Go board and a modern smartphone showing a chat bubble.](/images/ai-everyday-history.jpg)

**1950: a question.** Alan Turing asked, "Can machines think?" and proposed the Turing test: if a person chatting by text cannot tell a machine from a human, perhaps the machine is intelligent.

**1956: a name.** John McCarthy and colleagues organized a summer workshop at Dartmouth College, and the term "artificial intelligence" was born.

**1958 to 1966: first steps.** Frank Rosenblatt's perceptron learned simple patterns, an ancestor of today's neural networks. Joseph Weizenbaum's ELIZA chatted by matching keywords, the great-grandparent of modern chatbots.

**1970s and late 1980s: two "AI winters".** Promises ran ahead of what computers could do, and funding dried up twice.

**1997 to 2016: landmark wins.** IBM's Deep Blue beat chess champion Garry Kasparov in 1997. AlexNet started the deep learning boom in 2012, and DeepMind's AlphaGo beat Go champion Lee Sedol in 2016.

**2017 to today: AI for everyone.** The transformer, introduced in 2017, became the design behind today's language models. ChatGPT reached an estimated 100 million users within about two months of its 2022 launch, and AlphaFold's protein predictions earned a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

> The big shift: early AI followed rules that people wrote by hand. Modern AI learns patterns from examples. That is why it can handle messy tasks like understanding speech or spotting disease in a photo.

## Making daily life simpler

![Illustration of a city street where people use AI in daily life: a woman following a map app, a man reading live captions, a blind man using a phone camera to describe his surroundings, and a nurse photographing an older woman's eye.](/images/ai-everyday-daily-life.jpg)

Most helpful AI is not dramatic. It removes small frictions, many times a day.

### Getting around

Google Maps uses machine learning on live location data to predict traffic. Google and DeepMind reported up to 50% better arrival-time accuracy in some cities, which means less time stuck in traffic.

### Communication and language

- **Spam filtering:** Google says Gmail's machine learning filters block more than 99.9% of spam, phishing and malware before it reaches inboxes.
- **Translation:** apps like Google Translate can translate text from a phone camera in real time, so a traveller can read a street sign or a menu instantly.
- **Live captions:** phones from Apple, Google and Samsung can caption any audio on screen, helping people who are deaf or hard of hearing, or anyone watching a video in a quiet place.

### Accessibility

Microsoft's free Seeing AI app describes people, text and objects aloud for blind and low-vision users. Be My Eyes added an AI assistant that can read a medicine label or explain the buttons on a washing machine, so tasks that once needed help can be done independently.

### Health

In 2018 the US Food and Drug Administration authorized IDx-DR (now LumineticsCore), the first autonomous AI system to screen for diabetic eye disease. A nurse takes the photo and the patient gets a result in minutes, which matters in places with few eye doctors. Smartwatches can also flag irregular heart rhythms and prompt the wearer to see a doctor.

### Home and energy

Smart thermostats learn your routine and heat or cool the home only when needed. Google Nest reports average savings of roughly 10 to 12% on heating and about 15% on cooling: lower bills and lower energy use at once.

## Helping nature and the planet

![Illustration of AI protecting nature: a phone listening device on a rainforest tree, a camera trap photographing a zebra, a tractor spraying individual weeds, and a flood forecast map beside a river.](/images/ai-everyday-nature.jpg)

AI is also becoming a tool for protecting forests, wildlife, farmland and the climate.

- **Listening for illegal logging:** Rainforest Connection hangs recycled smartphones in rainforest trees. AI listens for chainsaws and alerts local rangers in real time.
- **Tracking wildlife:** Wildlife Insights sorts millions of camera-trap photos in a fraction of the time it would take by hand, and Wildbook recognizes individual whale sharks and zebras from their unique spots and stripes.
- **Smarter farming:** John Deere's See & Spray spots individual weeds and sprays only them, with reported herbicide savings of more than half. The PlantVillage Nuru app helps farmers in East Africa diagnose crop diseases from a phone photo, even offline.
- **Flood warnings:** Google's Flood Hub forecasts floods up to seven days ahead in more than 80 countries, giving families time to reach safety.
- **Cleaner traffic and buildings:** Google's Project Green Light tunes traffic-light timing, with early results of up to 10% lower emissions at intersections. DeepMind's AI cut the energy used to cool Google's data centres by up to 40%.

## How to use AI to improve your own quality of life

You do not need to be technical to benefit. Some simple starting points:

1. **Plan your day.** Ask an AI assistant to turn a messy to-do list into a schedule, or to suggest a weekly meal plan from what is already in your fridge, which also reduces food waste.
2. **Learn something new.** Ask a chatbot to explain a topic "like I am 12", then ask follow-up questions. Language apps like Duolingo use AI to adjust lessons to your level.
3. **Save energy.** Use a smart thermostat or smart plugs, and check your energy supplier's app for usage insights.
4. **Stay healthy.** Use step, sleep and heart-rate tracking as gentle reminders, but treat any health alert as a reason to talk to a professional, not as a diagnosis.
5. **Help nature.** Apps such as iNaturalist and Merlin Bird ID identify plants, insects and birds from a photo or a recording. Your observations also feed real scientific research.
6. **Make things accessible.** Turn on live captions, read-aloud and photo description features for yourself or a family member.

## Using AI wisely

AI is a powerful helper, but it is not perfect. A few habits keep it working for you:

- **Check important facts.** Chatbots can sound confident and still be wrong. Verify anything about health, money or law with a trusted source.
- **Protect your privacy.** Avoid sharing passwords, ID numbers or private medical details with AI tools.
- **Watch for bias.** AI learns from data made by people, so it can repeat unfair patterns. Good systems are tested across different groups of people.
- **Mind the footprint.** Training and running large models uses electricity and water. Use AI where it truly saves time or resources.
- **Keep humans in charge.** The best results come when AI handles the repetitive work and people make the decisions that need judgment and care.

## Glossary

- **Artificial intelligence (AI)**: Computer systems that perform tasks that usually need human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognizing images or making decisions.
- **Machine learning (ML)**: A way of building AI where the computer learns patterns from examples rather than following rules written by hand.
- **Neural network**: A machine learning model made of many simple connected units, loosely inspired by neurons in the brain.
- **Deep learning**: Machine learning with neural networks that have many layers. It powers modern speech, image and language AI.
- **Training data**: The examples a model learns from, such as labelled photos, recorded speech or text.
- **Algorithm**: A step-by-step set of instructions a computer follows to solve a problem.
- **Large language model (LLM)**: A model trained on huge amounts of text to understand and generate language. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are examples.
- **Transformer**: The neural network design, introduced in 2017, that most large language models are built on.
- **Computer vision**: AI that interprets images and video, used in weed-spotting sprayers, camera-trap sorting and eye-disease screening.
- **Natural language processing (NLP)**: AI that works with human language, including translation, captions and chatbots.
- **Generative AI**: AI that creates new content, such as text, images, music or code.
- **Turing test**: Alan Turing's 1950 proposal for judging machine intelligence: can a person tell the machine apart from a human in a text conversation?
- **AI winter**: A period when interest and funding in AI dropped because results did not match expectations. There were two, in the 1970s and late 1980s.
- **Bias (in AI)**: When a model's outputs are unfair to some groups, usually because of gaps or imbalances in its training data.
- **Hallucination**: When an AI system produces information that sounds plausible but is false.
- **Human in the loop**: A design where people review or approve AI decisions, especially when the stakes are high.

## The takeaway

AI began as a question about whether machines could think. Today a better question is how to use them well. From a nurse screening for eye disease to a ranger hearing a chainsaw miles away, the best examples share one thing: AI takes on the tedious or impossible part of the job so that people can focus on care, judgment and connection. Used thoughtfully, it can help both people and nature live better.
