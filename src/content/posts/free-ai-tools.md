There are lots of AI tools out there, and new ones arrive all the time. The good news: you don't need many to start.

Here are six friendly tools for everyday jobs. Each one has a free plan or free app, which I checked on the tool's own website in November 2026. You can start with any of them by simply typing a question.

## 1. ChatGPT: an all-round helper

**Best for:** questions, ideas, explanations and first drafts.

ChatGPT is a chat assistant from OpenAI. Its free plan includes unlimited text chats, plus a limited number of file uploads, image creations and voice chats.

> **Try this:** "I have eggs, spinach and half an onion. Suggest three quick dinners, with steps."

[chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com)

## 2. Google Gemini: help that works with Google apps

**Best for:** people who use Gmail, Google Docs, Maps or YouTube.

Gemini is Google's AI assistant, free with a Google Account. You can choose to turn on its connections to other Google services, such as Workspace, Maps and YouTube, and turn them off again.

> **Try this:** "Plan a relaxed Saturday in [your town] with a park, a café and a museum, and keep travel time short."

[gemini.google](https://gemini.google/overview/)

## 3. Microsoft Copilot: Microsoft's AI assistant

**Best for:** people who use Microsoft products.

Copilot is Microsoft's AI assistant. You can chat with it for free in your web browser or its app. Paid Microsoft 365 plans add higher usage and Copilot inside the Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook desktop apps.

> **Try this:** "Help me write a short, polite reply declining a meeting invitation."

[copilot.microsoft.com](https://copilot.microsoft.com)

## 4. Claude: chat, search and create files

**Best for:** writing, research and working through ideas.

Claude is a chat assistant from Anthropic. Its free plan works on the web, desktop and mobile. It can search the web, create files and run code, and it remembers context across conversations. Free use is limited every 5 hours and every week.

> **Try this:** "Turn these rough notes into a friendly one-page newsletter for my book club."

[claude.ai](https://claude.ai)

## 5. Gemini Notebook: learn from your own sources

**Best for:** students, book clubs and anyone with lots of reading.

Gemini Notebook is Google's notebook tool. (The old NotebookLM web address now leads here.) You add your own sources, such as PDFs, websites, YouTube videos, audio files, Google Docs or Slides, then chat with your notebook. Its answers include in-line citations, so you can see which source they came from.

It can also turn your sources into study guides, mind maps and **Audio Overviews**: discussions between AI hosts that summarise the key topics. It supports more than 80 languages, and you can use it without a paid plan.

> **Try this:** add a long report and ask, "What are the three main points, and what should I do next?"

[notebook.google](https://notebook.google)

## 6. Be My Eyes: help to see

**Best for:** blind and low-vision people, and anyone who supports them.

The free Be My Eyes app connects blind and low-vision people with real-time visual help from volunteers around the world. It also offers an AI assistant called Be My AI.

> **Try this:** if you have good eyesight, sign up as a volunteer and help someone when you're free.

[bemyeyes.com](https://www.bemyeyes.com)

## Also worth a look

**[Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai)** gives answers with a list of the sources it used, so you can check where information came from. Check its website for its current plans.

## How to choose

You really only need one or two to start:

| If you want to… | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Ask anything and get ideas | ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot or Claude |
| Study your own documents | Gemini Notebook |
| Check where an answer came from | Gemini Notebook (citations) or Perplexity (sources) |
| Get help seeing something | Be My Eyes |

## A few friendly safety tips

- **Double-check important facts**, especially about health, money or the law.
- **Keep private details private.** Avoid sharing passwords, bank details or other people's personal information.
- **Look at the settings.** Check what each tool can connect to, and turn off anything you don't need.
- **Free plans have limits.** That's fine for everyday use; you can decide later if a paid plan is worth it.

## Common questions

**Are these tools really free?**
Yes. Each tool's website lists a free plan or free app (checked in November 2026). Free plans have usage limits, and some features are kept for paid plans.

**Do I need to install anything?**
No for most of them: ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Claude and Gemini Notebook work in your web browser. Be My Eyes is designed for your phone's camera, and also has a desktop version.

**Which one is the best?**
The best tool is the one that fits your everyday job. Try one this week, and keep it if it saves you time or brings you joy.

## Try it this week

Pick **one** tool from this list and use it for one small task each day for a week. Notice what it does well and where you still prefer to do things yourself. Then come and tell me on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/thahira-syed-2827ba426/) which one you chose!
